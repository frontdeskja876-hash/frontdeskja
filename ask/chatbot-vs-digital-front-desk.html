The difference between a chatbot and a digital front desk

AI + business · 1 min read

“We have a chatbot” and “we have a digital front desk” sound similar and aren’t. A chatbot answers questions from a script. It’s useful for simple, repetitive queries — store hours, a shipping policy — and it tends to stall the moment a customer asks something it wasn’t scripted for.

A digital front desk is closer to what a good human receptionist actually does: it knows the business specifically (not a generic script), it can hold a real conversation rather than pattern-match to the nearest canned answer, it collects the right information from a customer (not just answers questions but qualifies what they need), and it can move someone toward an actual outcome — a booking, a structured request, a handoff to a person — rather than ending the interaction with “let me know if you have more questions.”

The practical test: when a chatbot hits the edge of its script, the conversation dies. When a digital front desk hits something it can’t resolve, it escalates to a person with the context already collected — the customer doesn’t have to start over. That’s the gap between a novelty widget on a website and something a business can actually run customer interactions through.
